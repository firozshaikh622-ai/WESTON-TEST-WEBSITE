Hiring a Contractor

Why the Cheapest Contractor Bid in New Town Is Rarely the Cheapest Outcome

By Firoz Shaikh, Founder • Kolkata • 6 min read

New Town's construction market has no shortage of small, local labour contractors willing to quote well below the going rate for a job. Some of that gap is genuinely a leaner cost structure — less overhead, direct labour relationships. Some of it is the first sign of a compromise that shows up later, usually after the work is already covered by the next layer of construction.

Where the Margin Actually Comes From

A quote significantly below competing bids for the same scope has to be making up the difference somewhere: thinner material specifications than the drawing calls for, informal or unskilled labour substituted for skilled trades on structural work, skipped curing time to keep the schedule moving, or under-declared quantities that get billed as "extras" once the work is already built. None of these show up in the initial quote — that's exactly why the quote can look attractive.

Why New Town Specifically Sees This Pattern

New Town's rapid build-out since HIDCO began allotting Action Area plots has pulled in a large pool of small contracting outfits competing directly on price, often for individual homeowners or small developers without an in-house engineering team to check work as it happens. Without independent verification, the gap between what was billed and what was actually built — in material grade, in quantity, in workmanship — often isn't visible until a crack, leak, or structural inspection surfaces it years later.

What Actually Protects You Isn't a Higher Quote

Paying more for a contractor doesn't guarantee quality either — it guarantees a bigger invoice, not necessarily better rebar cover or properly cured concrete. What actually protects you is independent verification: someone checking material grade against the BOQ before it's poured, tracking quantities against what's billed, and inspecting workmanship at each stage rather than only at final handover. That's a different spend than a higher contractor quote, and it's the one that actually catches a compromise before it's covered by the next slab.

The Real Question Isn't "Cheap or Expensive"

It's whether anyone independent of the contractor is checking that what's billed matches what's built. A contractor quoting fairly and getting checked, and a contractor quoting low with no independent oversight, are not the same risk — even if their invoices land at similar totals by the end of the project.

A low quote isn't a red flag by itself. A low quote with no independent verification of what's actually going into the structure is.
