PMC vs. CM

PMC vs. Construction Management: What's the Actual Difference?

By Firoz Shaikh, Founder • Kolkata • 6 min read

"PMC" and "construction management" get used almost interchangeably in the Kolkata market — the same firm might call itself either depending on who's asking. That's not necessarily dishonest, but it means the label alone won't tell you what you're actually hiring. The distinction that matters is whose interest the firm represents.

Construction Management (CM), Defined

Construction management, in its stricter sense, is focused on delivering the build itself — scheduling, means and methods, subcontractor management, and execution. Some CM arrangements (CM-at-risk) even have the construction manager holding the budget and construction risk directly, which puts them closer to a contractor than an independent advisor. A construction manager's success is usually measured by whether the building got delivered on schedule and within the budget they committed to.

Project Management Consultancy (PMC), Defined

A PMC's scope typically starts earlier and stays independent throughout — feasibility studies, design coordination, procurement advisory, and budget oversight, in addition to construction-stage supervision. Critically, a PMC doesn't hold construction risk itself; it represents the client's interest across whoever is actually building, whether that's one contractor or several. A PMC's success is measured by whether the client's interests — budget, quality, timeline — were protected, not by whether the PMC itself hit a number it committed to.

Why the Line Blurs in Practice

In the Indian market especially, firms use these terms loosely, and a "construction management" company may actually operate exactly like an independent PMC in practice, or vice versa. The title on the proposal isn't a reliable signal on its own.

The question to ask isn't "are you a PMC or a construction manager" — it's "whose interest are you representing, and what happens if I have a dispute with my contractor."

The Question to Actually Ask

Before signing with any firm using either label, ask directly: are you independent of the contractor doing the building, or affiliated with them? Do you hold any construction risk or budget commitment yourself? How is your fee structured — tied to construction cost, or independent of it? The answers matter far more than which term is on their letterhead.
