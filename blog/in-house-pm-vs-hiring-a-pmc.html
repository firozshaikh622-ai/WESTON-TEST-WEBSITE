In-House vs. Outsourced

In-House Project Management vs. Hiring a PMC

By Firoz Shaikh, Founder • Kolkata • 6 min read

Developers and builders scaling past their first project usually hit this decision at some point: build an internal project management team, or bring in an external PMC. Both work — the right answer depends on how many sites you're running and how consistently.

The Case for an In-House Team

An internal project management team gives you full control and institutional knowledge that accumulates project after project — the same people who managed your last site know exactly what went wrong and won't repeat it. This works best when you have a steady, multi-year pipeline that can justify fixed payroll year-round, regardless of how many sites are active at a given moment.

The Case for an External PMC

An external PMC comes with no fixed payroll cost — you pay for oversight only while a project is active. It also brings genuine independence: a PMC engaged directly by ownership has no internal politics to navigate and no incentive to protect an internal team's mistakes. And because a PMC works across multiple developers' sites, they bring outside benchmarking — they've seen what a reasonable BOQ, timeline, or defect rate looks like elsewhere, not just on your own projects.

Side by Side

AspectIn-House TeamExternal PMC
Cost structureFixed payroll, year-roundScoped to active projects
IndependenceInternal, reports up the same chainIndependent, accountable to ownership directly
Ramp-up timeHiring and onboarding takes monthsCan typically mobilize within weeks
Scaling across sitesRequires hiring ahead of pipelineScales up or down per engagement
Cross-project benchmarkingLimited to your own projectsDraws on multiple clients' sites

The Hybrid Model Most Kolkata Developers Actually Use

In practice, few developers run purely one way. The common pattern is a small internal team — often just one point person — for ownership-level decisions and approvals, with an external PMC handling the daily grind of site verification, contractor coordination, and reporting. That hybrid gets you continuity where it matters most (decision-making) without carrying full-time payroll for functions that only need to scale with active project volume.

The question isn't in-house or PMC — it's which parts of project management actually need to be in-house.
