Redevelopment vs. Renovation in Older Cities: What Howrah, Delhi, and Salt Lake Have in Common

By Firoz Shaikh, Founder • Kolkata • 6 min read

New Town's HIDCO-planned Action Areas get most of the redevelopment conversation in our market, but most of the built environment we actually work in — Howrah, Salt Lake, and now Delhi's established colonies through our Delhi NCR associate network — is older housing stock facing a different question entirely: repair it, or tear down and rebuild.

The Age Threshold Isn't the Only Variable

A 30-year-old building isn't automatically a renovation candidate, and a 40-year-old one isn't automatically a redevelopment candidate. What actually determines it: the condition of the structural frame (RCC carbonation and rebar corrosion, not just visible cracks), whether the plot's current FAR/FSI utilization is meaningfully below what current zoning allows (that gap is what makes redevelopment financially viable for a JV partner), and whether municipal infrastructure tie-ins — drainage, water, electrical load — can support the building's current use without an upgrade.

Older Municipal Corporations Add a Layer New Town Doesn't Have

Howrah Municipal Corporation and Delhi's DDA/MCD-regulated colonies both carry decades of incremental modification — unauthorized floor additions, altered setbacks, informal extensions — that HIDCO's newer Action Areas simply don't have yet. Before any redevelopment or major renovation decision, a structural and compliance audit needs to establish what's actually approved on record versus what's physically built, because that gap determines what a redevelopment plan can legally regularize versus what has to come down.

What This Means If You're Deciding Right Now

If you're a landowner in Howrah, an older Kolkata neighbourhood, or working through our Delhi NCR network, the honest first step is the same regardless of city: an independent structural assessment before talking to any developer about a JV, and a compliance check against municipal records before assuming your plot's FAR headroom is what you think it is. Both are things a PMC should do before a redevelopment conversation starts, not after a term sheet is on the table.

The building's age tells you to ask the question. The structural audit tells you the answer.
